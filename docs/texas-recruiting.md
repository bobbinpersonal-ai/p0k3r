# Recruiting 1099 sales reps: Craigslist, Facebook, Marketplace

Paste-ready copy, plus the rules that decide whether a post survives and
whether it quietly costs you a misclassification finding later.

Everything here points at **lovemeafter.com/sell**, which is live and writes an
applicant straight into the `Rep` table as `APPLIED`. Applications show up at
`/admin/tx/leads`.

---

## Before you post: three things that constrain the copy

**1. The ad is evidence in a classification dispute.** The Texas Workforce
Commission looks at direction and control, and it reads your job ad. Words like
*shifts*, *schedule*, *hourly*, *training programme*, *you'll report to*,
*team meetings every morning* are the exact language that turns an independent
contractor into an employee in hindsight — with back taxes and penalties on
every rep, not just the one who complained. Write *contract*, *your own hours*,
*your own vehicle*, *1099*.

**2. You cannot promise earnings you have not paid.** No "$150K first year", no
"our average rep makes $8,000 a month" — you have no reps and no averages. The
FTC treats unsubstantiated earnings claims as deceptive, and in this industry
they are the most-copied line in every ad. Show the *structure* and let the
maths speak: it is more credible than a number anyway.

**3. Never state a preference about a person.** No age, sex, national origin,
religion, disability, family status — including soft versions like "young and
hungry", "recent grads", "guys". "Bilingual Spanish a plus" is about the job
and is fine; "Hispanic preferred" is not.

> **Comp gap that limits every ad below.** `DEFAULT_BASE_COMMISSION_RATE` is
> still `0`, so as written you are offering a rep **nothing on the base price
> and 60% of whatever they sell above it**. That is a legal structure and a
> very hard sell to an experienced closer, who will ask what the base pays
> inside the first two minutes. Decide that number before you post, or expect
> the good applicants to disqualify you.

## Craigslist

**Category:** `jobs → sales / business development`. Not *gigs* — gigs are for
one-off work and a commission sales role posted there gets flagged. Job posts
are paid in Texas metros (roughly $25–45 per post per city, verify current
rates at post time) and run 30 days.

**Post separately per metro.** One post blanketing Dallas, Houston, Austin and
San Antonio is against their terms and gets ghosted. Pick the metro you can
actually service first.

**Craigslist requires commission-only roles to say so.** Bury it and the post
gets removed; lead with it and you filter for the people who want it.

### Title

```
Roofing / Exteriors Sales — Commission Only, 1099, Uncapped (Dallas–Fort Worth)
```

### Body

```
We're a residential exteriors company working Dallas–Fort Worth — roofing,
siding, windows, gutters, garage doors, fence and paint. Retail and insurance
restoration both.

We're looking for independent sales reps to run in-home appointments.

HOW THE MONEY WORKS

You see the price book. Not a marked-up version of it — the actual cost to us
and the actual base price on every line. You keep 60% of everything you sell
above that base.

That means your commission isn't a number we decide after the fact. It's
arithmetic you can do at the kitchen table, on a tablet, while the homeowner
is still deciding. Sell it at base, you make nothing on it. Sell it well and
you keep most of the difference.

There is no cap and there is no territory carve-up where someone else gets the
good half of the city.

WHAT THIS IS

- 1099 independent contractor. Not an employee.
- Your hours, your vehicle, your phone. You decide how you work.
- No base salary, no draw, no hourly.
- Commission is paid on money we've collected, within a week of collection.
- You're free to work for anyone else at the same time.

WHAT YOU NEED

- Reliable transport and a phone.
- Willingness to get on a roof or hold a ladder for someone who is.
- To be able to explain a contract to a homeowner without overpromising.

You do NOT need a contractor licence. Texas doesn't license roofing or
remodeling. You do not need prior roofing experience if you've closed in-home
before — we'll get you up to speed on product.

WHAT WE WON'T DO

We don't pay, rebate or absorb anybody's insurance deductible, and we don't
hire reps who offer it. Texas Business & Commerce Code 27.02 makes that a
crime, not a sales tactic. We don't act as public adjusters and we don't let
reps argue claims with carriers. If that's how you've been selling, we're not
a fit and it'll save us both a phone call.

APPLY

lovemeafter.com/sell — takes about two minutes.

Principals only. No agencies, no recruiters.
```

## Facebook

**Facebook Jobs no longer exists.** Meta retired the jobs product, so there is
no "post a job" flow to use and Marketplace's commerce rules are not written
for employment listings. What actually works now is **Groups**, and it works
better than Craigslist for this trade because the people you want are already
in them talking to each other.

### Groups to join first

Search and join before posting — most will reject a first-post ad from a
brand-new account:

- `Roofing Sales [your metro]`
- `Texas Roofing Contractors`
- `DFW Sales Jobs` / `Houston Sales Jobs`
- `Storm Restoration Sales`
- `[Metro] Side Hustles` and `[Metro] Jobs`
- Local neighbourhood and buy/sell/trade groups — lower quality, high volume

**Read each group's pinned rules.** Many allow promo only on a specific day.
Getting banned from the three good roofing-sales groups on day one is a real
cost; there aren't many of them.

### Group post

Shorter and more human than Craigslist. Groups punish copy that reads like an
ad.

```
DFW — looking for a couple of people to run in-home exteriors appointments.

Roofing, siding, windows, gutters, garage doors. Retail and insurance both.

Straight up on the comp because I know that's the first question: it's
commission only, 1099, and you see our actual cost and base price on every
line of the price book. You keep 60% of everything above base. No cap.

Your hours, your truck, your call on how you work. Work for someone else at
the same time if you want.

Two things I'll be upfront about — we don't touch anybody's deductible and we
don't argue claims with carriers. That's a crime in Texas and I'm not building
a business on it.

If you've closed in-home before, roofing or not, message me.
lovemeafter.com/sell
```

### Marketplace

Marketplace is for goods and services, not jobs, and job-shaped listings get
pulled. If you use it at all, list it as a **service you provide** — *"Free
roof inspection, DFW"* — and recruit from the people who reply asking whether
you're hiring. That's a real channel and it costs nothing, but it is a lead
channel with a recruiting side effect, not a recruiting channel.

Don't fight the policy. Groups are where the reps are.

## The reply template

Speed beats polish. Reply inside the hour or they've taken something else.

```
Thanks for reaching out.

Quick version: we're residential exteriors across DFW, retail and insurance.
You'd be running in-home appointments and writing contracts on our paper.

Comp is 60% of everything you sell above our base price, and you see the base
and our cost on every line so you always know what you're making. 1099, your
own hours, paid within a week of us collecting.

Two questions before we talk:
1. Have you closed in-home before? What was the ticket size?
2. Are you set up to work as a 1099 contractor (own vehicle, can invoice)?

If that all lines up, fill this in and I'll call you:
lovemeafter.com/sell
```

## Screening: four questions that do the work

Ask these on the phone screen. They're on the application at `/sell` too.

1. **"Walk me through the last deal you closed in someone's house."**
   Real closers narrate the room. People who have never done it describe a
   product.

2. **"A homeowner says they can't afford the deductible. What do you say?"**
   The only acceptable answers involve financing, scope, or walking away. Any
   version of *"we'll take care of it"* ends the interview — § 27.02 is
   criminal and one rep saying it in one kitchen is your problem, not theirs.

3. **"What do you do when the adjuster lowballs the scope?"**
   Correct: show them the damage, document it, let the homeowner deal with
   their carrier. Wrong: anything where the rep negotiates the claim — that's
   Ch. 4102 and it's the single most common thing storm reps do wrong.

4. **"How many doors would you knock in a week if we gave you no leads?"**
   You have no lead flow yet. The honest answer to this question is the whole
   job, and anyone who doesn't want to hear it will quit in three weeks.

## What to have ready before the first rep says yes

- [ ] Base commission rate decided (`src/lib/texas/commission.ts`)
- [ ] Rep agreement printed — `/admin/tx/paperwork?doc=rep`
- [ ] W-9 collected. **No W-9, no payout.** The `Rep.w9OnFile` flag gates it.
- [ ] Price book carrying your real supplier numbers, not seed rates
- [ ] `NEXT_PUBLIC_TX_PHONE` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_BUSINESS_ADDRESS` set, or the
      homeowner contract you hand them is defective
- [ ] A crew who can actually build what they sell
