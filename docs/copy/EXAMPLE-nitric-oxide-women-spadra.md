## Long version (What's Inside)

**Arginine**
The body has always made its own vasodilator; it just needed the raw material.
Arginine is the amino acid the body converts into nitric oxide, the molecule
that signals blood vessels to relax and widen. That pathway is the reason
nitric oxide research won a Nobel Prize in 1998 — and the reason this pack is
built around it.

**Citrulline**
Watermelon rind, the part most people throw away, is one of nature's richest
sources. Modern work found the reason it matters: citrulline survives the gut
and liver better than arginine does, and is converted to arginine downstream —
so it's the more efficient way to feed the same nitric oxide pathway.

**Fenugreek**
Ground into porridge on the Nile and into curries across South Asia for
thousands of years, valued as a warming, restorative seed. Standardised
fenugreek extracts are among the more heavily studied botanicals in modern
libido and hormonal-balance research, in both men and women.

**DIM**
The compound your body makes for itself every time you eat broccoli, kale or
cabbage — the reason those plants earned their reputation long before anyone
could explain it. Research on DIM centres on how the body metabolises
oestrogen, which is why it sits in a women's formula rather than a generic one.

**Magnesium**
Mineral-rich spring water and dark leafy greens were the old sources; most
modern diets supply noticeably less. Magnesium is a cofactor in hundreds of
enzyme reactions, including those governing muscle relaxation and vascular
tone — quiet, foundational, and one of the most common shortfalls in Western
intake surveys.

**NAC**
The precursor to glutathione, the body's own master antioxidant. NAC has been
on the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines for decades — an unusually strong
pedigree for something sold on a supplement shelf.
